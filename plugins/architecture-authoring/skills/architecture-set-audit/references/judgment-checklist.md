# Judgment checklist

What the script cannot see. Work through every item, quote the evidence with file and line, and order findings by damage.

1. **An inference that acquired an owner.** An `[I]` whose sentence, or a later one, names a person who will confirm or close it. Either it is an open item and moves to the register, or the owner is removed.
2. **An open item restated as fact.** A statement that matches an `[A-nn]` row but appears elsewhere without citing it, usually two or three sections after the row. Cite the row or close it on evidence.
3. **A thin section that acquired a target.** A measurable target in runtime (§6) or quality requirements (§10) that no client statement supports.
4. **A terminology pair used interchangeably.** The glossary declares two words distinct (published and live, estimated and billed); search for each and read every use. Catalog item 10 started as one word, "indicative", spread across three meanings.
5. **A derivative behind its sources.** A brief whose pinned versions trail the living documents means the derivative is stale and is regenerated, never patched.
6. **A figure stated two ways.** Contingency, buffered milestones, team size, or a count stated differently in two places.
7. **A pre-drafted section merged over a generated one.** A response template's own assumptions section placed where the projection from the registers belongs.
8. **An absorption promise.** "If an input is missing, we will cover it" in a fixed-fee offer, with no schedule consequence stated.
9. **A diagram element with no source.** An arrow or box in an asset that no brief element and no register row supports.
10. **A count with no list anywhere.** "Eleven surfaces" stated and never enumerated in the pack.
11. **Audience leakage by paraphrase.** Content from an excluded section (commercial constraints, staffing) restated in a candidate section in other words, which the derived check cannot catch.
